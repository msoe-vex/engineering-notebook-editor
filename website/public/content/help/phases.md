# The Design Process

Following a structured design process is a core requirement for a high-scoring engineering notebook.

## 1. Define Problem

**What to include:**

- **Problem/Design statement**
  - Problem Statement: What is the issue or need you are addressing?
  - Design Statement: What are you trying to create or improve?
- **SMART Goals**
  - Specific: What exactly do you want to achieve?
  - Measurable: How will you know when you've achieved it?
  - Achievable: Is it realistic given your resources and constraints?
  - Relevant: Does it align with your overall objectives?
  - Time-bound: When do you plan to achieve it?
- **Criteria & Constraints**
  - Criteria: What are the requirements your solution must meet?
  - Constraints: What limitations (time, materials, budget) do you have?
- **Deliverables and Deadlines**
  - Deliverables: What are the tangible outputs (CAD models, prototypes, test results)?
  - Deadlines: When are these deliverables due?
  - Use a Gantt chart or timeline to visually represent your project schedule and milestones. (See the Resources section for templates and examples)

> **Tip:** Be as specific as possible and realistic. If you don't define the problem clearly, your solution might solve the wrong thing.

## 2. Generate Concepts

**What to include:**

- **Research & Citations**
  - Summarize relevant research, existing solutions, and inspiration.
  - Include citations for any sources you reference.
- **Brainstorming sessions**
  - Document your brainstorming process, including all ideas generated (even the bad ones).
- **Sketches and CAD concepts**
  - Include rough sketches and preliminary CAD models of your ideas.
- **Decision Matrix**
  - Create a decision matrix to objectively evaluate your concepts against your criteria and constraints. (See the Resources section for templates and examples)
  - Include 3-5 attributes and rate each concept on a scale (e.g., 1-10) for each attribute. Calculate total scores to identify the most promising concept.
  - Include 3-5 concepts in your matrix to show a range of options.
  - Use a weighted decision matrix if some criteria are more important than others, assigning weights to each attribute accordingly.

> **Tip:** Use a decision matrix to objectively choose the best path forward. You don't have to choose the highest-scoring concept, but you should explain your reasoning if you deviate from the matrix results.

## 3. Develop Solution

**What to include:**

- **Detailed CAD & Sketches**
  - Include detailed CAD models with dimensions and annotations.
- **Math calculations and physics justifications**
  - Show all relevant calculations that justify your design choices (e.g., projectile motion, kinematics).
- **Pseudocode and Flowcharts**
  - If your solution includes software or control systems, include pseudocode for complex algorithms or flowcharts to explain your logic.
- **Clear annotations explaining design choices**

> **Tip:** This is your blueprint. Someone should be able to build your design just by looking at this section.

## 4. Construct & Test

**What to include:**

- **Build pictures with annotations**
  - Document your construction process with photos and detailed annotations explaining each step.
  - This could include code snippets for programming, wiring diagrams for electronics, or step-by-step assembly instructions.
- **Parts list and instructions**
  - Include a comprehensive parts list with quantities and sources.
  - Provide overview of instructions for how to build your design.
- **Test Plan**
  - Test cases with expected outcomes
  - Data collection methods
  - Test results with analysis
- **Quantitative Data**
  - Tables summarizing your test data
  - Charts and graphs to visualize performance metrics (e.g., velocity vs. time, accuracy comparisons)
- **Brief analysis of results**
  - Discuss whether your design met the criteria and constraints based on your test data, but save your full analysis for the next section.

> **Tip:** Document your failures just as much as your successes. Use quantitative data to back up your conclusions, not just qualitative observations.

## 5. Evaluate Solution

**What to include:**

- **Reflection on Criteria/Constraints**
  - Analyze how well your solution met each of your criteria and constraints based on your test results.
- **Recommended initial improvements**
  - Based on your evaluation, suggest specific improvements you would make if you had more time or resources. This can become the basis for the next iteration of your design.
- **Event reflections and feedback**
  - If this is after a competition or event, reflect on your performance, including what went well and what could be improved.
  - Include any feedback you received from judges, mentors, or peers.
- **Final conclusion**
  - Summarize your overall findings and the success of your design in addressing the problem statement.

> **Tip:** Be honest and critical in your evaluation. This is where you demonstrate your engineering judgment and ability to learn from the design process.
